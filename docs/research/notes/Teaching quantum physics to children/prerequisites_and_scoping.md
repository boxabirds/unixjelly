# The prerequisite problem in teaching quantum physics to children aged 9 to 14

Note on evidence level: almost all measured-outcome evidence found is from learners aged 14 to 18. The only studies of children 11 or younger found (Einstein-First Year 6, Year 3 to 10 curriculum) report mostly attitudes, or results pooled with relativity and gravity. Every claim about 9 to 12 year olds below is an extrapolation unless stated otherwise.

## Which layers are truly required for a first, correct quantum model, and which are historical baggage? (quantum first vs history first)

### Takeaway
Expert consensus (two Delphi studies) puts interference, superposition and measurement at the core, taught conceptually, not mathematically. Curricula still mostly follow the historical route (energy levels, photoelectric effect, de Broglie, Heisenberg). Whether the Bohr model is harmful is disputed, and I found no head-to-head trial of "quantum first" against "history first" at any age.

### Cited Findings
- Stadermann, van den Berg and Goedhart (2019) analysed official upper-secondary curricula from 15 countries (including the UK, Australia, Germany, the Netherlands). The shared core has seven categories: discrete atomic energy levels, light–matter interaction, wave–particle duality, de Broglie wavelength, technical applications, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, and the probabilistic nature of quantum physics. Interpretations and epistemology appear in only a few countries. — [Stadermann et al. 2019, PRPER 15, 010130](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.15.010130); [APS Physics synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v12/s61); [VU research portal](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/analysis-of-secondary-school-quantum-physics-curricula-of-15-diff/)
- The authors recommend putting more weight on process (nature of science) than on facts. — [APS Physics synopsis](https://physics.aps.org/articles/v12/s61)
- Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. (2019) ran a Delphi study with experts in quantum physics, mathematics, chemistry and biophysics. Duality, wave functions and atoms were ranked most important for secondary school. Topics ranked lower were often judged too difficult. — [Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2019, Int. J. Sci. Educ., doi 10.1080/09500693.2018.1550273 (repository record)](https://utrecht-main-test.atmire.com/items/12982779-7b63-4621-96a1-f45ac7e9b4df)
- Bitzenbauer, Ubben, Anttila, Krijtenburg-Lewerissa and 13 others (2025) ran a three-round adapted Delphi study. Experts strongly supported quantum interference, superposition and quantum measurement as the foundation, "at a conceptual rather than a mathematical level". More advanced quantum-technology topics were widely judged unsuitable for secondary school. — [Bitzenbauer et al. 2025, PRPER 21, 020157](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/d1yx-kfsz)
- A conference abstract for the same study gives the panel as 175 experts (physics teachers, physics education researchers, quantum physicists). The PRPER abstract I fetched does not state the size. — [CERN Indico, QUANT 2025 session](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1175859/contributions/6400786/)
- The national curricula Stadermann et al. analysed follow the historical route. Many European secondary standards take the historical approach. Italian guidelines, for example, include the Bohr model next to the photoelectric effect and the uncertainty principle. — [QTris paper, arXiv 2608.09430](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09430)
- Against the Bohr model: one source argues that the textbook story of early atomic models does not match the real history. — [Textbook myths about early atomic models, arXiv 2212.08572](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08572). Another calls the Bohr model qualitatively wrong as a picture of the atom. — [Hees, "sins" lecture notes, Frankfurt](https://itp.uni-frankfurt.de/%7Ehees/gen-phys/sins.pdf) (opinion piece, not research).
- For the Bohr model: McKagan, Perkins and Wieman argue that the evidence for "Bohr inhibits learning" is weak. They say the model should still be taught, better: as a model, set against the quantum model. — [McKagan, Perkins & Wieman, arXiv 0707.1541](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0707.1541) (undergraduate context)
- Misconceptions are attributed to classical and quantum frameworks getting mixed. Proposed fix: treat them as separate systems and set the semiclassical model against the modern one explicitly. — [Chem. Ed. review, chemedu.cfu.ac.ir](https://chemedu.cfu.ac.ir/article_4452.html) (review of 2016 to 2023 work, in Persian with English abstract; I did not read it in full)
- Mixed classical/quantum thinking also depends on the physical context chosen. In a cluster-randomised field study (n = 181, upper secondary), students taught with which-path single photons kept mixed frameworks more often than students taught with polarisation or a double well. — [Tóth et al. 2025, PRPER 21(2), via FAU spotlight](https://www.lightmatter.fau.de/2026/02/fau-lmq-research-spotlight-teaching-and-learning-quantum-physics-via-2-state-approaches-the-context-matters)
- Einstein-First argues explicitly against "you need classical physics first". Its Part 3 paper names the "belief that to understand modern physics students need to have a strong background in classical" physics as the assumption it tests. — [Kaur, Blair et al., Teaching Einsteinian Physics at Schools Part 3, arXiv 1707.03729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03729)

### Inferences
- A defensible minimal core for a first correct model is: (1) outcomes are discrete and random, (2) probabilities are predictable, (3) superposition and interference (amplitudes that can cancel, unlike probabilities), (4) measurement changes the state. Black-body radiation, the Bohr orbit and the de Broglie formula are not needed for any of these. They are in curricula for historical reasons, not because a correct model depends on them.
- "Atoms" and "energy levels" are needed only if the course explains spectra, lasers or chemistry. A qubit or polarisation course can leave them out entirely.
- The Tóth et al. result suggests the *choice of two-state system* matters as much as the history-or-not question. Which-path photon interferometers, a common "quantum first" entry, invited more classical-particle thinking than polarisation did.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study comparing a quantum-first sequence with a historical sequence, at any age.
- I did not verify the well-known Munich course (Müller and Wiesner, around 2002, which drops the history and starts from single-photon experiments). It is not cited here because I did not fetch a source.
- I also did not fetch the Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2017 PRPER review of secondary and lower-undergraduate quantum teaching, so I cannot report its findings.

## How specific programmes scope what they teach

### Takeaway
Each successful programme picks one narrow physical system and one representation, then leaves out everything that representation doesn't need. Quantum Picturalism drops all linear algebra and keeps only adding angles. Einstein-First drops classical prerequisites and uses physical models (Nerf guns for photons). Spin First uses electron spin as the single two-state system. QKD activities need only polarisation and probability. The outcome claims are mostly from pilots without a control group.

### Cited Findings
**Quantum Picturalism (QPic) / ZX-calculus (Coecke, Quantinuum, Oxford)**
- The 2023 pilot had 54 UK students aged 16 to 18, randomly selected from 734 volunteers, with no advanced maths prerequisites. Training was 16 hours over eight weeks. 82% passed the end-of-course exam and 48% earned a distinction. — [Dündar-Coecke et al., arXiv 2504.01013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01013v1); [Coecke et al., "High schoolers excel at Oxford quantum course using pictorial mathematics", arXiv 2512.00141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00141v1), also in [J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 3189, 012008 (2026)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/37914/1/Coecke_2026_J._Phys.__Conf._Ser._3189_012008.pdf)
- Format: weekly pre-recorded video lectures, hour-long live online tutorials (groups of up to 15), 12 homework exercises, and a take-home exam with three weeks to complete it. Topics: states and processes, gates and circuits, Bell states, teleportation, entanglement, mixed states, measurement-based computing. Left out: the Schrödinger equation and detailed algorithms. — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1)
- Maths replaced: complex matrices, linear maps and matrix multiplication give way to diagram-rewrite rules (fusion, copying and so on). The authors say only "the ability to express angles in degrees and add them together" is needed. — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1). The earlier paper also lists vectors, tensors, complex numbers and trigonometry as avoided. — [arXiv 2504.01013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01013v1)
- **The claim that they "outperformed Oxford students" is not supported by the paper.** The exam was *adapted from* past Oxford postgraduate exams and double-marked by Oxford staff. The paper reports no Oxford cohort sitting the same exam and no Oxford comparison numbers. There is no control group. Results were also filtered with a "canary question", and students who failed it were excluded. — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1)
- Possible conflict of interest: most authors list Quantinuum first and developed QPic themselves. — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1). Secondary coverage repeats the headline without these caveats. — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/?p=2377047); [Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/82-percent-quantum-high-schoolers-excel-oxford-course-demonstrating-success-pictorial/)
- The paper says the maths needed is no more than UK pupils learn at 7 to 11, but presents no data from that age group. It mentions future work with Colorado schools to "examine the quantum concepts learnable by even younger age groups". — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1)

**Einstein-First (Australia; Blair, Kaur, Zadnik et al., University of Western Australia)**
- The programme runs from Year 3 to Year 10 (ages 7 to 16). The curriculum paper reports a mixed-methods study in six schools with 315 students, designed with the Model of Educational Reconstruction. — [Developing and implementing an Einsteinian science curriculum from Years 3 to 10, Part A, arXiv 2306.17342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17342)
- The quantum module uses models and analogies: photons as particles (a Nerf gun model) and thought experiments. — [Teaching Einsteinian Physics at Schools Part 2, arXiv 1707.03728](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03728); [Einstein-First programme page, OzGrav](https://outreach.ozgrav.org/portal2/programs/einstein-first/)
- Outcomes (Part 3): tested with Years 6, 9, 10 and 11 (ages 11 to 16). Year 9 (academically talented, n = 57, 20 lessons in 2014, "light, space and time"): pre-test fewer than 10% scored above 50%; post-test 53 of 57 scored 80% or more; class average rose from 23% to 91%. Year 6 (n = 26, attitudes only): 54% said they were not too young, about 19% said they were too young, and about 27% were undecided. Girls' positive attitude towards physics rose from 50% to 80%. — [Kaur et al., Part 3, arXiv 1707.03729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03729)
- Weaknesses: the samples were selected talented students, there was no control group, the testers were the programme developers, and quantum results are pooled with relativity and gravity, so they are not reported separately. — [Kaur et al., Part 3, arXiv 1707.03729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03729)
- A later abstract reports trials in more than 20 schools with attitude gains, especially for girls, but gives no quantitative data. — [Blair & Kaur, ICPE 2022 abstract](https://openjournals.library.sydney.edu.au/ICPE/article/view/17012/14567). A programme slide deck claims about 1,600 pre/post records and a Year 5 "Fantastic Photons" module (undated, unpublished). — [Einstein-First slides (Google Slides)](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1UZRrXRU5QowkW0ecJUxORpAkZcdYSAu7/htmlpresent)
- UWA press release: "Einstein not too hard for school students". — [UWA news, 2021](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2021/august/einstein-not-too-hard-for-school-students) (press release)

**Spin First / two-state systems first**
- Spin First (EPJ Quantum Technology, published November 2025) is a qualitative lower-secondary concept. Electron spin is the model two-state system, linked to quantum computing ideas. It was evaluated with grade 9 students (about 14 to 15) in semi-structured individual interviews (a related record gives N = 11). Findings: students appear to understand the foundational principles qualitatively, with no significant mixing of classical and quantum principles. Problems found: language and verbal reasoning, hidden-variable reasoning, and inert or missing prior knowledge. — [EPJ Quantum Technology 2025, doi 10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00436-0](https://epjqt.epj.org/articles/epjqt/abs/2025/01/40507_2025_Article_436/40507_2025_Article_436.html); [TU Dresden record](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/developing-a-quantum-physics-curriculum-for-lower-secondary-education-insights-into-the-design-of-a-spin-first-teaching-concept-and-first-empirical-findings(d008bdec-3dff-48b5-b080-31f2ef952580).html)
- Two-state approaches can use polarisation, spin, a double well, or other systems with two basis states. — [Pilot projects in QT education, arXiv 2303.07055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.07055)
- Michelini's group taught Dirac's approach through light polarisation to 24 Hungarian high school students. — [Education Sciences, Sep 2022 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/db428105c3754e95906c505246d50e3c)
- Tóth et al. 2025 ran a cluster-randomised quasi-experiment with 181 upper-secondary students across three two-state contexts. All groups improved. The polarisation and double-well groups gained significantly more than the which-path single-photon group. — [FAU spotlight on Tóth et al., PRPER 2025](https://www.lightmatter.fau.de/2026/02/fau-lmq-research-spotlight-teaching-and-learning-quantum-physics-via-2-state-approaches-the-context-matters); [APS PRPER highlights](https://journals.aps.org/prper/highlights) (I read only abstract-level summaries, not effect sizes.)

**Quantum computing first**
- A 6th-grade study (Kim and Menekse, ASEE 2026, work in progress) had 201 students and two teachers. It folded wave–particle duality and energy quantisation into the existing standards. Nine-item test scores rose from mean 2.015 to 3.445 (t(199) = 11.876, p < .001). There was no control group, and the topic is light, not qubits. — [ASEE 2026 paper](https://nemo.asee.org/public/conferences/374/papers/51374/view)
- Design-based middle-school work orders the topics as a sequence from radioactive decay (randomness) to quantum computing. — [Akdemir, Purdue dissertation](https://hammer.purdue.edu/articles/thesis/_b_Design_Implementation_and_Evaluation_of_a_Quantum-Infused_Middle-School_Level_Science_Teaching_and_Learning_Sequence_b_/26322175) (outcomes not retrieved)
- A quantum drug-discovery lesson for middle and high school is a design proof of concept, not an outcome study. — [Farris, ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/quantum-computing-at-the-intersection-of-engineering-technology-science-and-societal-need-design-of-ngss-aligned-quantum-drug-discovery-lessons-for-middle-school-students)
- Bitzenbauer et al. 2025 report that experts judged advanced quantum-technology topics unsuitable for secondary school. That is a caution for qubit-first courses that go as far as algorithms. — [PRPER 21, 020157](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/d1yx-kfsz)

**Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) as an entry point**
- One study assessed 12th-grade students' conceptual and quantitative understanding and motivation after QKD teaching, and notes that "teaching QKD in high school is yet understudied". — [EPJ Quantum Technology, s40507-024-00276-4](https://epjquantumtechnology.springeropen.com/articles/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-024-00276-4)
- A pre-university BB84 workshop (about three hours of lectures plus a three-hour activity) deliberately builds in a simplification that leaves a security hole, which students then exploit. — [Utama, Lee, Seidler, arXiv 1911.02565](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1911.02565v2)
- Unplugged versions exist: "BB84: Quantum Coins" role play with coins and boxes (Alice, Bob, Eve, "nature"). — [Waterloo IQC activity](https://uwaterloo.ca/institute-for-quantum-computing/sites/default/files/uploads/files/02-qcrypto-coins-student.pdf). There is also a Thorlabs-kit lesson over three 50-minute periods. — [NSTA Science Teacher 2021](https://www.nsta.org/science-teacher/science-teacher-januaryfebruary-2021/your-eyes-only)
- A BB84 lesson sequence uses learning outcomes such as judging the effect of eavesdropping with probability. — [Bringing the second quantum revolution into high school, arXiv 2206.15264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15264)

### Inferences
- The common scoping pattern: pick one two-state system (polarisation is the best supported), teach measurement and probability on it, and add interference and entanglement only through that same system. The programmes with the strongest numbers (QPic, Einstein-First Year 9) both used self-selected or talented students. Expect smaller effects with whole classes of 9 to 14 year olds.
- QKD needs only polarisation (or a coin and box analogy), basis choice, and probability. It is the entry with the fewest prerequisites, and it suits children who already know about passwords and eavesdroppers. But it teaches "measurement disturbs" without interference, so on its own it never reaches the core idea of amplitudes.

### Gaps
- No QPic data below age 16. Einstein-First's 2023 Years 3 to 10 outcome numbers were not in the abstract, and I did not read the full PDF.
- No controlled outcome study of a qubit-first course for ages 9 to 14 was found.
- No controlled evaluation of a school BB84 activity was found.

## What maths can be replaced by diagrams, arrows or probability games, and what is lost?

### Takeaway
Diagrams (QPic) replace complex linear algebra and tensor products while staying rigorous. Feynman's "little arrows" (phasors, sum over paths) replace complex numbers with rotating arrows and still give interference correctly. Coin and box games replace probability notation. What gets lost is continuous systems (the Schrödinger equation, wave functions, atoms) and a path to standard notation. Analogies also bring risks, such as hidden-variable reasoning.

### Cited Findings
- QPic replaces matrices, linear maps, tensors, complex numbers and trigonometry with diagram rules plus adding angles in degrees. The course leaves out the Schrödinger equation and algorithms. — [arXiv 2512.00141 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00141v1); [arXiv 2504.01013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01013v1)
- Sum over paths (Feynman's arrows) was used for the quantum chapter of the UK Advancing Physics AS course (2000). Ogborn, and Hanc and Taylor, have argued for it at high school level. — [Malgieri et al., IJPCE](https://ijpce.org/index.php/IJPCE/article/download/19/27/95)
- In an Italian high school sequence, a sum-over-paths explanation of wave–particle duality "may be effective" in helping students build consistent mental models. The group was only 14 students. — [Malgieri, Onorato, De Ambrosis, PRPER 2017 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/713f6331c84240c085a755449682909b); [Malgieri, ECU 2021](https://sciforum.net/paper/9314)
- Tactile "phasor wheels" can add arrows to get photon probability amplitudes, reportedly trialled with 14 to 15 year olds. — [ECU 2021 paper, doi 10.3390/ecu2021-09314](https://doi.org/10.3390/ecu2021-09314) (only a search summary was read; weak)
- Circuit boards that mimic quantum gates were built for upper-secondary students who don't know the formalism. The paper reports experience, not measured outcomes. — [Karlstad group, via search results; Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1729099/full) (the attribution between these items is unclear in the search summary; treat with care)
- One 2025 entanglement sequence moves from a magnet analogy to the Bloch sphere to Qiskit simulation. — [Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1729099/full)
- A known risk of analogies: Spin First found reasoning with hidden variables among 14 to 15 year olds. — [EPJ QT 2025 Spin First](https://epjqt.epj.org/articles/epjqt/abs/2025/01/40507_2025_Article_436/40507_2025_Article_436.html). Students also confuse how superposition relates to entanglement (entanglement implies superposition, not the other way round). — [Investigating student understanding of quantum entanglement, arXiv 1512.02346](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.02346) (older students)
- Coin and box BB84 replaces states with physical tokens. — [Waterloo IQC activity](https://uwaterloo.ca/institute-for-quantum-computing/sites/default/files/uploads/files/02-qcrypto-coins-student.pdf)

### Inferences
- Arrows are the cheapest substitute that keeps the hardest idea, *negative and cancelling contributions*. Probability games cannot show interference, because classical probabilities never cancel. A course for 9 to 14 year olds built only on coins and dice would teach randomness and measurement but not what is truly quantum.
- QPic keeps rigour but needs angle addition, which UK pupils meet at about 9 to 11. That puts the "add angles" prerequisite within reach of the 9 to 14 range, but this is untested.
- Lost in every substitute: continuous wave functions, atoms and energy levels, and the move to standard notation. The Delphi experts ranked wave functions and atoms highly, which conflicts with qubit-only scoping. — [Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2019](https://utrecht-main-test.atmire.com/items/12982779-7b63-4621-96a1-f45ac7e9b4df)

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring what students later lose (for example, trouble moving to Dirac notation) after a diagram-only or arrows-only course.
- I found no direct evaluation of Ogborn's Advancing Physics sum-over-paths material.

## Is there evidence about the minimum age at which each idea works?

### Takeaway
There is effectively none for specific quantum ideas below about age 14. The youngest measured quantum-relevant results are Einstein-First Year 6 (age 11; attitudes only, pooled with relativity) and a 6th-grade wave–particle study (modest gains, no control). Evidence for superposition and measurement starts at grade 9 (Spin First, N about 11, interviews). Entanglement evidence is from 16 to 18 year olds (QPic).

### Cited Findings
- Year 6 (age 11, n = 26), Einstein-First: attitudes only. 54% said they were not too young, about 19% said they were too young. — [Kaur et al., arXiv 1707.03729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03729)
- Before teaching, only about 10% of 11 year olds and about 25% of selected 16 year olds scored about 50% on Einsteinian concepts. Prior knowledge is low at every age. — [Kaur et al., arXiv 1707.03729](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03729)
- "Can 8-year-olds understand concepts of Einsteinian physics?" exists (Einstein-First), but from its title and companion work it appears to concern gravity and space-time, not quantum. — [arXiv 2203.00842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00842); [Physics for the masses: Einsteinian gravity in primary school, arXiv 2109.00598](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.00598) (not read in full)
- Grade 6 (about 11 to 12, n = 201): wave–particle duality and energy quantisation; mean score rose from 2.0 to 3.4 out of 9. — [ASEE 2026](https://nemo.asee.org/public/conferences/374/papers/51374/view)
- Grade 9 (about 14 to 15): qualitative understanding of spin superposition and measurement, in interviews. — [EPJ QT 2025 Spin First](https://epjqt.epj.org/articles/epjqt/abs/2025/01/40507_2025_Article_436/40507_2025_Article_436.html)
- Ages 16 to 18: entanglement, teleportation and mixed states via QPic. — [arXiv 2504.01013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01013v1)
- Phasor arrows: 14 to 15 year olds, reportedly. — [ECU 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/ecu2021-09314)

### Inferences
- Rough evidence floor by idea: quantised light (photons as particles) at about 11 (Einstein-First; weak). Superposition and measurement on a two-state system at about 14 (Spin First; very small n). Interference with arrows at about 14 to 15 (weak). Entanglement at about 16 (QPic; pilot). For 9 to 13 year olds, every quantum idea is untested in controlled studies.
- Children's understanding of probability is a likely limiting prerequisite at 9 to 11, but I found no quantum-specific study of it.

### Gaps
- I found no developmental-psychology study on the age at which children can reason about amplitudes or non-classical correlations.
- I did not open the Seven-Category Framework for K-12 Quantum Education ([arXiv 2512.18886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18886)). It may hold age-band recommendations.

## Published concept maps, frameworks and learning progressions

### Takeaway
The European Competence Framework for Quantum Technologies (QTEdu/QUCATS) targets higher education and industry. It is not a school progression. For schools, the closest things to concept maps are the two Delphi rankings and Stadermann's seven-category curriculum core. I found no validated learning progression for ages 9 to 14.

### Cited Findings
- The European Competence Framework for Quantum Technologies (CFQT) is by Greinert, Müller and others, within the Quantum Flagship projects QTEdu and QUCATS. Version 2.5 (April 2024) adds a "proficiency triangle": six levels (A1 to C2) across three areas, roughly concepts and theory, engineering, and applications and strategy (names vary between sources). It also adds nine qualification profiles drawn from 34 industry interviews. Its scope is education and training for the QT workforce, not schools. — [CFQT v2.5 PDF](https://qt.eu/media/pdf/Competence_Framework_for_QT_v2_5_2024.pdf); [Greinert et al., arXiv 2410.07692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07692v1); [TU Braunschweig news](https://magazin.tu-braunschweig.de/en/m-post/roadmap-for-quantum-careers-around-the-world)
- Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2019 Delphi: duality, wave functions and atoms were ranked top for secondary school. — [repository record](https://utrecht-main-test.atmire.com/items/12982779-7b63-4621-96a1-f45ac7e9b4df)
- Bitzenbauer et al. 2025 Delphi: interference, superposition and measurement, taught conceptually. Advanced quantum technology unsuitable. — [PRPER 21, 020157](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/d1yx-kfsz)
- Stadermann et al. 2019: the seven-category shared core across 15 countries. — [PRPER 15, 010130](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.15.010130)
- Einstein-First sequences concepts across Years 3 to 10 using the Model of Educational Reconstruction. — [arXiv 2306.17342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17342)
- A 2023 summary of EU Quantum Flagship pilot projects on QT education covers two-state approaches. — [arXiv 2303.07055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.07055)

### Inferences
- The shift between the two Delphi studies (2019: duality, wave functions, atoms; 2025: interference, superposition, measurement) suggests expert opinion is moving from the historical, wave-mechanics core towards a two-state, quantum-information core. Different panels and questions limit how firm this comparison is.
- A 9 to 14 course would have to build its own progression. The Einstein-First Years 3 to 10 sequence is the only published age-spanning one found, and its quantum strand is mainly about photons.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the 2019 Delphi's full ranked list or the 2025 panel breakdown.
- I did not read the Krijtenburg-Lewerissa et al. 2017 review of secondary quantum teaching.
- I found no school-level version of the CFQT.
